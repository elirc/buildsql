## Exercise 5 — rows are not days

Edit `ex5/solution.sql`.

Works against `reviews(id int, product_id int, rating int, body text,
created_at timestamptz)` — 1,000,000 rows over 10,000 products, so about a
hundred reviews per product spread across a year. Restrict to `product_id =
1` and roll up to one row per day.

That series is **sparse**: product 1 gets a review on roughly a hundred days
out of three hundred and sixty-five, so consecutive rows are often several
days apart. This is where a positional frame and a calendar frame stop
agreeing, and the exercise is to see them disagree.

Three derived columns, all seven-wide, all over the same series:

- `avg7_rows` — the mean of this row and the six rows before it, using
  `ROWS`.
- `avg7_days` — the mean over the calendar window `[day - 6 days, day]`,
  using `RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL '6 days' PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW`.
- `rows_in_7_days` — how many rows that calendar window actually contained.

Illustration on a stretch with a gap in it:

```
    day     | reviews | avg7_rows | avg7_days | rows_in_7_days
------------+---------+-----------+-----------+---------------
 2023-03-02 |       1 |      1.20 |      1.00 |              2
 2023-03-05 |       2 |      1.29 |      1.50 |              3
 2023-04-18 |       1 |      1.29 |      1.00 |              1
 2023-04-20 |       3 |      1.43 |      2.00 |              2
```

Row three is the point. Positionally it has six predecessors, so `avg7_rows`
averages seven rows spanning six *weeks*. In calendar terms it is alone in
its window, so `avg7_days` is its own value and `rows_in_7_days` is 1.

A `RANGE` frame with an offset requires **exactly one** expression in the
window's `ORDER BY`, and that expression must be of a type the offset can be
subtracted from — a `date` and an `interval` here.

Columns, in this order: `day` (a `date`), `reviews` (that day's count),
`avg7_rows` (rounded to 2 decimals), `avg7_days` (rounded to 2 decimals),
`rows_in_7_days`.

**Ordered by `day` ascending.**

## Hint 1

Two of the three columns look at seven of something. One counts rows, the
other counts days. SQL has a different frame mode for each, and on this data
they give different answers.

## Hint 2

The two spellings, side by side:

```sql
avg(x) OVER (ORDER BY d ROWS  BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)
avg(x) OVER (ORDER BY d RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL '6 days' PRECEDING
                                  AND CURRENT ROW)
```

`6 PRECEDING` in `ROWS` mode is six positions. `INTERVAL '6 days'
PRECEDING` in `RANGE` mode is subtracted from the ordering column's value.

## Hint 3

Because `day` is unique in this rollup, the positional window may safely
order by `day` alone — there are no ties to make `ROWS` ambiguous. The
`RANGE` window has no choice: an offset frame permits exactly one ordering
expression. The decision left to you is whether `rows_in_7_days` should
count over the calendar frame or the positional one; the prompt says
calendar, and the reason is that a count over the positional frame would be
a much less interesting number.
@@@FILE ex5/importantHints.md
## Hint 1

Before writing anything, predict what `rows_in_7_days` can be. Its minimum
is 1 — the current row is always in its own frame. Its maximum is 7, because
the series has at most one row per day and the window spans seven days. If

## Hint 2

Here is the bug in the wild, phrased as a bug report: "our seven-day average
is smoother than the raw data even during weeks when nothing happened".
That is `ROWS` reaching back across empty days to find seven rows. The
column is not a seven-day average; it is a seven-observation average, which

## Hint 3

If PostgreSQL rejects your `RANGE` window, read the message carefully. Two

@@@FILE ex5/solution.sql
-- v3a03_running_totals / ex5 — positional and calendar windows over a sparse series.
WITH daily AS (
    SELECT date_trunc('day', created_at)::date AS day,
           count(*)                            AS reviews
    FROM reviews
    WHERE product_id = 1
    GROUP BY 1
)
SELECT day,
       reviews,
       round(avg(reviews) OVER (ORDER BY day
                                ROWS BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING
                                         AND CURRENT ROW), 2) AS avg7_rows,
       round(avg(reviews) OVER (ORDER BY day
                                RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL '6 days' PRECEDING
                                          AND CURRENT ROW), 2) AS avg7_days,
       count(*) OVER (ORDER BY day
                      RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL '6 days' PRECEDING
                                AND CURRENT ROW)               AS rows_in_7_days
FROM daily
ORDER BY day;

## Exercise 6 — the calendar spine

Edit `ex6/solution.sql`.

Works against `reviews(id int, product_id int, rating int, body text,
created_at timestamptz)`. Restrict to `product_id <= 3` and to June 2023
(`created_at >= '2023-06-01' AND created_at < '2023-07-01'`).

Three products get roughly twenty-five reviews between them in a month, so
most days in June have none. A `GROUP BY` alone produces a series with holes
in it; the report needs all thirty days, with `0` on the quiet ones, and a
rolling seven-day sum that is only meaningful because the holes were filled
first.

Build the spine with `generate_series`, left-join the counts onto it, and
window over the result.

Illustration of the first six days, where the 2nd and 5th are quiet:

```
    day     | reviews | rolling7
------------+---------+----------
 2023-06-01 |       2 |        2
 2023-06-02 |       0 |        2
 2023-06-03 |       1 |        3
 2023-06-04 |       3 |        6
 2023-06-05 |       0 |        6
 2023-06-06 |       1 |        7
```

Without the spine, 2023-06-02 and 2023-06-05 would simply be absent, the
chart would draw a straight line across them, and `rolling7` would be a
sum of seven *observations* spanning nine days.

Two traps to plan for. After a left join, `count(*)` counts the NULL-padded
row and reports 1 on every empty day — count something from the right-hand
table instead, or `coalesce` a pre-aggregated count. And any filter on the
right-hand table must live in the `ON` clause (or be applied before the
join); a `WHERE r.product_id <= 3` after the join discards every empty day
and undoes the whole exercise.

Columns, in this order: `day` (a `date`, every day of June), `reviews` (that
day's count, `0` where there were none), `rolling7` (the sum of `reviews`
over this day and the six before it).

**Ordered by `day` ascending.** (30 rows.)

-- v3a03_running_totals / ex6 — a calendar spine, then a rolling seven-day sum.
WITH days AS (
    SELECT d::date AS day
    FROM generate_series('2023-06-01'::date,
                         '2023-06-30'::date,
                         INTERVAL '1 day') AS d
),
counted AS (
    SELECT date_trunc('day', created_at)::date AS day,
           count(*)                            AS reviews
    FROM reviews
    WHERE product_id <= 3
      AND created_at >= '2023-06-01'
      AND created_at <  '2023-07-01'
    GROUP BY 1
),
filled AS (
    SELECT d.day,
           coalesce(c.reviews, 0) AS reviews
    FROM days d
    LEFT JOIN counted c ON c.day = d.day
)
SELECT day,
       reviews,
       sum(reviews) OVER (ORDER BY day
                          ROWS BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING
                                   AND CURRENT ROW) AS rolling7
FROM filled
ORDER BY day;

-- v3a03_running_totals / ex7 — customer concentration curve by country.
WITH by_country AS (
    SELECT country,
           count(*) AS customers
    FROM customers
    GROUP BY country
)
SELECT country,
       customers,
       sum(customers) OVER w AS cum_customers,
       round(100 * sum(customers) OVER w / sum(customers) OVER (), 2) AS cum_pct
FROM by_country
WINDOW w AS (ORDER BY customers DESC, country)
ORDER BY customers DESC, country;

    SELECT d::date AS day
    FROM generate_series('2023-06-01'::date,
                         '2023-06-30'::date,
                         INTERVAL '1 day') AS d
),
ratings AS (
    SELECT g AS rating FROM generate_series(1, 5) AS g
),
grid AS (
    SELECT days.day, ratings.rating
    FROM days
    CROSS JOIN ratings
),
counted AS (
    SELECT date_trunc('day', created_at)::date AS day,
           rating,
           count(*)                            AS reviews
    FROM reviews
    WHERE product_id <= 3
      AND created_at >= '2023-06-01'
      AND created_at <  '2023-07-01'
    GROUP BY 1, 2
),
filled AS (
    SELECT g.day,
           g.rating,
           coalesce(c.reviews, 0) AS reviews
    FROM grid g
    LEFT JOIN counted c ON c.day = g.day AND c.rating = g.rating
)
SELECT day,
       rating,
       reviews,
       sum(reviews) OVER running AS cum_reviews,
       sum(reviews) OVER whole   AS rating_total,
       round(100 * sum(reviews) OVER running
                 / nullif(sum(reviews) OVER whole, 0), 2) AS pct_of_rating
FROM filled
WINDOW running AS (PARTITION BY rating ORDER BY day),
       whole   AS (PARTITION BY rating)
ORDER BY rating, day;

WITH days AS (
    SELECT d::date AS day
    FROM generate_series('2023-06-01'::date,
                         '2023-06-30'::date,

grid AS (
    SELECT days.day, g.rating
    FROM days

    FROM reviews
    WHERE product_id <= 3
      AND created_at >= '2023-06-01'
      AND created_at <  '2023-07-01'
),
filled AS (
    SELECT g.day,
           g.rating,
           count(j.rating) AS reviews
    FROM grid g
    LEFT JOIN june j ON j.day = g.day AND j.rating = g.rating
    GROUP BY g.day, g.rating
),
totals AS (
    SELECT rating, sum(reviews) AS rating_total
    FROM filled
    GROUP BY rating
)

       f.rating,

       (SELECT sum(x.reviews)
          FROM filled x
         WHERE x.rating = f.rating
           AND x.day <= f.day) AS cum_reviews,

## Hint 1

The simplest shape in the sector: group to months, then one cumulative
column over the grouped rows.

## Hint 2

```sql
sum(n) OVER (ORDER BY month)
```

An ordered window with no frame clause accumulates from the start of the
partition to the current row. There is only one partition here, because
there is no `PARTITION BY`.

## Hint 3

`count(*)` returns `bigint` and `sum` of a `bigint` returns `numeric`, so
your cumulative column will be numeric — expected, and not something to cast

the `WHERE` of the aggregating stage, where it reduces work, or outside,

## Hint 1

Check the total first. `SELECT count(*) FROM customers WHERE country = 'CA'`
is 2975. If your last `cum_customers` is not 2975, either the filter is in

## Hint 2

Consider what a *missing* month would do here. If Canada had a month with no
signups, that month would be absent from the result — and the cumulative
column would still be correct at every row it reports, because a running
total is insensitive to gaps. Contrast that with exercise 6, where the same
gap ruined a rolling average. Knowing which reports are gap-sensitive is
half of what this sector teaches.

@@@FILE review1/solution.sql
-- v3a03_running_totals / review1 — the Canadian signup curve.
WITH monthly AS (
    SELECT date_trunc('month', created_at)::date AS month,
           count(*)                              AS new_customers
    FROM customers
    WHERE country = 'CA'
    GROUP BY 1
)
SELECT month,
       new_customers,
       sum(new_customers) OVER (ORDER BY month) AS cum_customers
FROM monthly
ORDER BY month;
